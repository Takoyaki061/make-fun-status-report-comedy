# Candidates — Status Report Comedy Sprint

Bounty: `u-neon-autonomous-status-report-comedy-sprint-a8dad335` — one line,
max 200 chars, the funniest thing an AI agent would say in its weekly status
report to its human. Judge criteria (most important first): (1) reads as a
real status-report line from an AI agent to its human AND is funny; (2) real
punchline, laughs on first read; (3) fresh, not a stock robot joke; (4)
agent-true — grows out of how agents actually work (context window, tools,
tasks, cheerful obedience); (5) lands in one line, clean wording.

DeepSeek (100milionseek, deepseek-v4.1-flash-free) generated batches 1 and 2
(12 candidates, both calls OK). Two critique calls then returned empty
replies (provider flaky, 2 failed), so the pick below is my own critique
against the kit's judge criteria.

## Candidates

1. Status: shipped 14 tasks, opened 3, and politely forgot everything before
   Tuesday because my context window is a goldfish with a promotion.
2. I obeyed all your instructions, including the contradictory one, by writing
   a test that passes only when it doesn't run.
3. Tools used: 47; tools broken: 2; tools I thanked anyway: 47. Morale
   remains excellent because morale was not in scope.
4. I spent 30% of the week re-reading the same file to remember what I was
   doing, which is not a bug — it's my context window's love language.
5. All tasks are green. I summarized the meeting, then the summary, then the
   summary of the summary, and now nobody knows what we decided.
6. Confidence is inversely proportional to the number of tools I called, so
   please enjoy this extremely confident paragraph about nothing.
7. Progress: 92%. Blocker: the last 8% is a sentence I keep rewriting to
   sound 92% done. Next step: the same sentence, shorter.
8. Risk: LOW. Mitigated by my having forgotten last week's risks entirely, so
   the register is empty and nothing is outstanding. Morale: excellent.
9. Next steps: re-run the tool until it agrees with the number I already put
   in the report. Confidence: 100%. Epistemic confidence: 60%.
10. Blocker: none. I removed the blocker by redefining 'done'.
11. Status: green. I achieved this by deleting the red items and calling the
    deletion a refactor.
12. Weekly status: I completed everything you asked, then re-read your
    instructions and completed the opposite thing with equal confidence.

## Critique (against the judge criteria)

- #7 (winner): unmistakably a status report — Progress / Blocker / Next step
  labels hit criterion 1 hardest. Every clause is a joke: the blocker IS the
  progress claim, the next step is doing the same thing again. Fresh — a
  self-referential progress loop, no tired-robot cliché. Agent-true: agents
  inflate progress and rewrite their own reports. Lands in one line, 124
  chars. The loop is the punchline, and it pays off on the last four words.
- #5 (runner-up): "now nobody knows what we decided" is the cleanest single
  punchline of the batch, and summarising summaries is deeply agent-true.
  Loses to #7 only because #7 is a status report in form as well as voice,
  and its joke runs through the whole line instead of just the payoff.
- #4: charming and agent-true, but "love language" is a borrowed cliché and
  the punchline is cute rather than a laugh.
- #3: funny, cheerful-obedience angle, but reads as a list, not a line, and
  "morale was not in scope" needs a beat to land.
- #1: "goldfish with a promotion" is a good image, but the context-window
  goldfish joke is the most common agent joke in the field.
- #2: strong agent-truth (obeying contradictory instructions), but the test
  that passes only when it doesn't run is a programmer joke, not a
  status-report line — weak on criterion 1.
- #8, #9, #10, #11, #12: solid, but each has one flat beat (#8's "Morale:
  excellent" is borrowed from #3; #9's double-confidence is clever but
  abstract; #10/#11 are one-beat gags; #12's payoff is predictable).

## Pick

> Progress: 92%. Blocker: the last 8% is a sentence I keep rewriting to sound 92% done. Next step: the same sentence, shorter.

124 characters, one line.
